<h2> </h2>
<h6><strong>JOHN CALVIN</strong> (Presbyterian)</h6>
<h6>“The word ‘baptism’ signifies to immerse. It is certain that immersion… was the practice of the Primitive Church.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>MARTIN LUTHER</strong> (Lutheran)</h6>
<h6>“Baptism is a Greek word and may be translated ‘Immerse.’ I would have those who are to be baptized, be altogether dipped.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>JOHN WESLEY</strong> (Methodist) </h6>
<h6>“The Bible term ‘Buried with Him by Baptism’ alludes to the ancient manner of baptizing by immersion.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>WALL</strong> (Episcopal) </h6>
<h6>“Immersion was in all probability the way in which our blessed Saviour, and for certain, the way in which all early Christians were baptized.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>BRENNER</strong> (Catholic) </h6>
<h6>“For the first thirteen hundred years, baptism was an immersion of the person under water.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>STOUDZA</strong> (A native Greek) </h6>
<h6>“The verb ‘baptize’ has only one meaning. It signifies to plunge. Baptism and immersion are identical. To say baptism by sprinkling would be to say immersion by sprinkling.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>JEREMIAH</strong> (A Greek Patriot) </h6>
<h6>“The ancients did not sprinkle the candidate, but immersed him.”</h6>
<h6> </h6>
<h6><strong>WHITFIELD</strong> (Methodist) </h6>
<h6>“It is certain that the word ‘buried’ in the text, Romans 6:4 alludes to the matter of baptizing by immersion.”</h6>
